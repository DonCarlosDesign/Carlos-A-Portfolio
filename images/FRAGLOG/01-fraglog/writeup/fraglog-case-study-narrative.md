# FRAG.LOG — Case Study Narrative

For use on the doncarlosdesign.com project page. Written in DCD's voice, framed for a client/visitor audience — not an internal build log.

---

## The brief

FRAG.LOG needed a stats-tracking product for competitive FPS players that didn't look like everything else in the category. Most tools in this space default to the same visual shorthand: neon-on-black, purple-blue gradients, glowing UI chrome borrowed from esports broadcast graphics. It reads as "gaming," but it doesn't read as *trustworthy* — and a stats tool lives or dies on whether players believe the numbers.

## The approach

Instead of designing another esports dashboard, we looked at how serious data tools actually present information — trading terminals, flight boards, order books. Dense grids. Minimal chrome. Numbers that speak for themselves without needing decoration to feel important.

We paired that discipline with one deliberate exception: a marquee moment for the numbers that matter most — current rank, win rate, K/D — treated like a scoreboard, not a spreadsheet cell. The result sits between two references that don't usually meet: a trading desk and a high-score screen. Terminal, with one earned flash of arcade.

## Key decisions

**Color as a signal, not a mood.** Most competitors reach for a broad, decorative palette — the more colors, the more "energetic" it's supposed to feel. We went the opposite direction: one accent color (amber), reserved strictly for wins, MVP performances, and hero numbers. Everything else stays neutral. When a player sees amber on screen, it means something happened — it isn't wallpaper. A separate, quieter tier system (gray, blue, purple) handles day-to-day performance grading, so the one loud color never gets diluted into meaninglessness.

**A voice that reports, not cheers.** The product doesn't congratulate you. No exclamation points, no "Nice job!" — match results and milestones are written the way a system log would write them: lowercase, factual, present tense. "1,000,000 rounds logged." not "You did it!" That restraint is what makes the amber accent land when it does show up — it's earned by everything around it staying quiet.

## The system

Every screen, ad, and brand asset in this project pulls from a single locked design system — palette, type, spacing, and voice rules defined once and applied consistently across five app screens, a five-piece ad campaign, and the wordmark itself. Nothing here is a one-off; it's a product identity built to hold up under real use.

## The handoff

The final deliverable: a fully coded, working prototype — not static mockups — built from the design system by Claude Code, ready to demonstrate the product exactly as it was designed.
